Bounty — Concept A: Scoreboard

DESIGN MOCKUP — every number is illustrative, nothing is read from the API · Hyperwolf Bounty — name picked by the owner 2026-09-07; one incentive is a bounty
Concept A · organizing idea

The module is not a set of screens with a leaderboard on one of them. The module is a board — one wall-sized standings surface a store can put on the back-room screen and leave up all day.

Everything a budtender cares about is on that one screen at once: where they sit, who moved past them since the last sync, how their store is doing against the other four, what each live bounty needs from them, and money landing in a ticker along the bottom. Nothing is a click away, because a board that needs clicking is a board nobody looks at. Rank movement is the concept’s one genuinely new idea — an arrow against the previous sync, so the board rewards a glance rather than a study.

Manager work never takes the board over. Bounty builder, approvals, identity binding, goal overrides and record-paid all open in a right-hand Sheet (window.Sheet, the Engage kit’s 460px drawer) with the board still visible and still updating behind it — the manager is a floor manager standing at the same screen the staff are watching, not a person who left for the back office. Learn (Snaps) is a strip of story circles across the top of the board with an unread ring; the player is a centred overlay so the board is what you come back to. Ingestion lives in a sources dock in the board’s footer that expands upward when you need it, so freshness is permanently in eyeline and the full Data screen is the dock opened all the way.

Who it serves best. The floor during a shift, and the store as a group. A budtender gets one glance and knows what to sell next; a manager gets approval and identity work done without losing the room’s attention. What it trades away: it is the least form-friendly of the four. A ranked bounty with three prize places, a brand filter, an hourly recurrence and a funding cap is a long form crammed into a 470–560px drawer with a lot of vertical scroll — Concept A accepts that cost deliberately, and the builder tab shows exactly how bad it gets. Deep analysis (cohorts, month-over-month, per-brand breakdowns) also has nowhere natural to live: the board answers “right now”, not “why”.

What it borrows from, by name. The density and column discipline come from pos/screen-orders.jsx — the fulfilment kanban, where five stages sit side by side on one screen and the operator is expected to read all of them at once without scrolling. The stat blocks come from pos/screen-home.jsx’s local Stat (surface2 fill, 3px accent left border, 10px uppercase label, mono value) and its CardHead row. The standings table follows window.DataTable’s convention exactly, the way pos/screen-aov.jsx’s hand-rolled leaderboard already does. The Sheet, StatTile and toast come from the Engage kit (shared/hd-ui.jsx), which means the new app’s entry HTML loads hd-format.jsx + hd-ui.jsx the way Hyperwolf Engage.html does — the POS Home card and Register strip on tab 9 use POS atoms only, because those two files are not loaded on the POS page.

Composites built visibly out of atoms, named where they appear: the standings table is DataTable; the store race is a stack of BarMeter in a Card padding=0; a bounty lane is a Card + Pill + four BarMeters; the ticker is a scrolling row of mono ·-separated lines in a Card; the sources dock is Card + Pill rows; a story circle is Avatar inside a 2.5px ring div. No new primitive is invented anywhere in this concept.

Where this concept is honest by construction
  • The freshness line sits above the standings, not under it. A rank you cannot date is a rank you cannot trust, and the board is read from the top.
  • The unattributed row is inside the table, in warnSoft, ranked nowhere — it is money the standings cannot see, so it is shown where the standings are read.
  • The sources dock is permanent chrome. A store whose Blaze sync died at 09:14 sees a red dot in the footer of a board that is already on the wall.
  • Every reward figure on the board carries projected or settled. Nothing here pays anyone; the module tracks and records only.
Screen · #/ · the landing surface

One board. Standings, the store race, every live bounty and the money, without a scroll on a 1280-wide screen.

Period and metric are two Seg controls in the board header, not a filter panel — changing them re-ranks in place. Scope (This store / All stores) is a third. Rank uses competition ranking (1, 1, 3): two people tied at $3,410 both take rank 3 and the next person is rank 5, with a tied marker on both rows because a tie changes what a prize pays. People with no sales yet are listed by name under the table at rank null — never rank-last at zero.

Screen · #/contests and #/contests/:id

The bounty ledger takes the board’s left column; a bounty you open takes the right-hand Sheet. The board never goes away, and neither does the freshness line the standings inside the Sheet depend on.

Six statuses, all present, all reachable from one list: draft, pending approval, active, ended, settled, cancelled. active and ended are derived from the clock, not from a stored transition, so a bounty that ran out at midnight is already labelled “ended” without anybody pressing anything. settled is a real write and pays into the points ledger once per (bounty, round, person). Approving a brand-funded bounty is the one action here that changes what money the store owes, so it sits alone in the Sheet footer in accent.

Screen · #/contests/new · the concept’s honest weak spot

Everything a bounty needs, in a 560px drawer, over roughly two and a half screens of scroll. This is what Concept A trades away, drawn at full length rather than hidden.

The one mitigation the concept allows itself: a permanent “What this pays” summary pinned above the footer, computed from whatever is filled in so far, so the manager never has to scroll back up to check what they just built. Kind is chosen first because it decides which of the later blocks exist at all — a store-vs-store bounty has no participant list, an AOV goal has no reward places.

Screen · #/earnings · the ticker, opened

The bottom ticker is the door. Opening it turns the board’s footer into the full ledger — yours by default, the store’s if you manage it, with projected and settled kept visibly apart everywhere.

Projected is what a bounty currently says you would earn if it ended now; it moves with the ledger and is not a promise. Settled is a written row in inc_points, one per bounty, round and person, and it never changes again. The two are never summed into a single headline figure. Recorded paid is a bookkeeping marker — who marked it, when, and how they say they paid — and it moves no money; that is a stated escalation in §11 of the plan, and this screen is drawn as if the marker is kept.

Screen · #/learn · the strip and the player

Snaps live where they are actually useful: across the top of the board, as story circles with an unread ring. The player is a centred overlay over the board, so finishing a snap puts you straight back on the standings.

Cards advance on tap — right half forward, left half back — with a progress bar segment per card. A quiz card is the only card that blocks: it waits for an answer, shows whether you were right, and if the snap carries a reward, a pass writes one inc_points row and the ticker at the bottom of the board picks it up. A snap can be linked to a bounty, so “learn it” sits beside “sell it”. Authoring is a manager task, so it opens in the right-hand Sheet like everything else.

Screen · #/data · the dock, opened all the way

The sources dock is always in the board’s footer. This screen is that dock pushed open — the same five rows, now with their run history, their rejects, their conflicts and the identities nobody has matched yet.

The invariant the backend enforces is printed on every run report: rows read = inserted + unchanged + conflicts + rejected. If those five numbers do not add up, the report says so rather than rounding the difference away. A re-upload of a file already ingested is unchanged, not a duplicate; the same transaction key arriving with a different content hash is a conflict that keeps the stored row and asks a person. Identities are never bound on a name — name similarity only proposes.

Screen · #/goals · the absorbed AOV goal

AOV goals stop being their own feature and become one bounty kind — a standing goal with no window, no prize and no ranking, whose reward unit is recognition.

That is the whole absorption: the same store-default-plus-overrides shape, the same required reason, the same append-only audit, now sitting in the bounty list alongside the spiffs and inheriting the board’s freshness line. The /api/aov/* contract is unchanged and served from the new ledger, so pos/screen-aov.jsx keeps working until the day the POS card is swapped. Because a goal is a bounty kind, a budtender sees it on the board as a meter rather than as a separate card they have to remember to open.
