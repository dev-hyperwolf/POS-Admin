Current flow — the problem, reproduced

Two separate defects, and this tab reproduces both

The scroll-to-top jump: the trigger ("Bindings" / "Pick a node") lives inside a table row, which can be a long way down the page — the real screen stacks an explainer, a source banner, a KPI row, and three more report cards above the table before a single category row appears. The editor itself renders in a fixed spot in the page — right after the KPI row, near the very top — regardless of which row you clicked. Scrolling it into view (the existing fix) works exactly as coded: it snaps the whole page back up near the top, away from the row you were just looking at. That jump is the fix behaving correctly for the wrong layout.

Pick a node, then there's a bind button: the node picker is its own scrollable box; the actual "Add this node" button is in a second block below it that only exists after an async preview call returns, and it stays disabled until you retype the exact number the preview showed. Three waits and a typed number between "I picked it" and "it's bound."

Direction A — a drawer that takes over, in place

The editor stops living in the page's document flow

"Bindings" now opens a fixed-position panel anchored to the viewport, not to a spot in the page. It doesn't matter whether the row is 200px down or 2,000px down — the drawer appears in the same place, the page behind it never scrolls, and closing it costs nothing: you're exactly where you left off. Inside, the picker and the confirm-and-bind step sit in the same panel, one directly under the other, so selecting a node and confirming the bind read as one continuous action instead of two sections separated by a network round trip.

Direction B — a persistent side panel

Table and editor share the screen instead of trading places

The table gets its own scroll region on the left; a binding panel sits fixed on the right for the whole session. Nothing opens or closes — clicking "Bindings" on any row just swaps what the panel shows, instantly, with no transition to watch and no position to lose. You can compare two categories' bindings back to back without ever losing the table under you. This direction costs the most layout space and reads best on a wide screen; on narrow viewports the panel drops below the table instead of beside it.

Click "Bindings (2)" on Flower, then click "Pick a node" on Vapes — watch the panel update without the table ever losing your place.
