Concept A · organizing idea

Verify is a console, not a feature. It is the shape your team already knows — a left rail with every screen on it, a dense table you can sort and filter, and a session page with the pictures on the left and the machine’s reasoning on the right.

You have run identity checks through Didit for two years. Nobody needs to be taught a new metaphor to keep doing it. Concept A takes that layout — the one Didit, Persona, Veriff and Sumsub all converge on because it is the layout the work actually has — and rebuilds it in Hyperwolf’s own tokens, on Hyperwolf’s own engine, with the parts that were opaque made legible: every decision node says what it measured, what the threshold was, and whether the model behind it is certified.

What it optimises. Completeness and density. Eleven rail items means there is no screen you have to go to Didit for, and no setting that lives only in a config file. A row in the verifications table carries the session number, the person, the document type, the workflow, the status, the reason codes and the timestamp without truncating any of them, so an analyst working a backlog reads it like a ledger. The session page puts front, back, selfie, portrait crop and the similar-faces strip in one column and eight decision cards in the other, so the answer to “why did this go to review” is on screen without a single click.

Who it serves best. The person who does this all day. An analyst clearing a queue, a manager auditing last week’s declines, an engineer wiring the site to the API. It is deliberately unexciting: familiar chrome, keyboard shortcuts, exportable tables. The customer-facing capture flow (tab 7) is the only part that is not a console — that one is a phone, and it is designed like a phone.

Composites built visibly out of atoms, named where they appear: the verifications table is DataTable with SortableTH from shared/hd-ui.jsx; a status pill is Pill with the ten Didit literals mapped onto kinds; a decision node is Card + Pill + BarMeter; the filter row is MultiSelectFilter chips; the review queue rows are DataTable with a Sheet for the detail; thresholds are DualRange/native range with Switch; the capture frames are plain Cards at phone width. No new primitive is invented anywhere in this concept.
What this concept optimisesranked
1 · Zero relearning
Same shape as Didit
Rail on the left, table in the middle, two-pane session detail. Your analysts open it and work. The only new vocabulary is the reason codes, and those are printed on the row.
2 · Density
11 columns, 34px rows
A screen shows about 24 sessions without scrolling. Filters are chips, not a modal. Sorting is a click on the header. Export is a button, not a support ticket.
3 · Completeness
Nothing lives elsewhere
Workflows, questionnaires, lists, keys, webhooks, usage, retention, audit — all eleven Didit surfaces have a home here, so nothing forces you back into a vendor console you are trying to leave.
4 · Legibility of the machine
Every score shows its threshold
A node never says just “pass”. It says the measured value, the workflow threshold it was compared against, the model that produced it, and whether that model is certified. Liveness says uncertified everywhere it appears.
What you give up
  • It is a screen for experts. Eleven rail items and a nine-column table is intimidating on day one. A store manager who opens Verify twice a month will not find “why was this customer declined” faster than they would in a two-card summary — Concepts B–D each buy that back at a cost this one refuses to pay.
  • Review throughput is table-shaped, not queue-shaped. Clearing a backlog means clicking a row, deciding, going back. Tab 5 adds keyboard shortcuts to soften it, but the console never becomes a one-item-at-a-time machine the way a dedicated review surface would.
  • It inherits Didit’s information architecture, including the parts that are wrong for you. Questionnaires and Customization each get a rail item you may never open, while the thing you actually do every day — the register check-in — is a modal reached from another app entirely.
  • Mobile is a compromise. The table reflows and the two-pane stacks, but nobody is clearing a review queue on a phone in this concept. The capture flow is phone-first; the console is not.

The one honest asterisk

Didit’s engine is certified for presentation-attack detection. Yours will not be on day one. Concept A does not hide that behind a green tick: liveness scores carry an open model · uncertified caption everywhere they render, imported sessions are labelled Verified by Didit, imported and are excluded from accuracy panels, and the Usage screen prints “no per-check fee (own engine)” rather than inventing a price.

The eleven rail items, and what each one is forfull IA
Home
Needs-review tile first, then volume, conversion, resubmissions, warnings, locations, devices. Engine health badge sits in the header, not a panel.

Only what is waiting on a person, with priority, assignment and keyboard-first decisions.

The customer directory. One person, many sessions, one face template set, a KYC expiry date.

Extra questions attached to a workflow. Imported from Didit so a migrated workflow round-trips.
Customization
What the customer sees: brand name, logo, accent token, per-step copy, hosted domain.
Lists
Eleven list types, blocklists and custom. A hit is a warning with the list id, never a silent decline.
Integrate
API keys, webhook destinations, the delivery log, code snippets, hosted link, and a Didit-compatible facade.
Usage
Per-feature counts with an honest cost column. Cumulative and this-month, side by side.
Settings
Team and roles, audit log, retention and deletion, engine health detail. One line saying there is no login yet.
Businesses (KYB)
Didit has it. You do not do business verification, so it is not built — noted here so the parity gap is deliberate rather than forgotten.
The ten statuses, exactly as Didit spells themcase-sensitive

The literals are the contract. Your site reads status off the decision object today and branches on the string, so the console shows the string it will receive — including In Review, which the compatibility facade returns unsoftened. What a row is doing

The session number is Didit's numbering continued, so #14145 is still #14145 after the import and the newest native session starts at #14146. That matters when someone quotes a number from an old email. The Didit tag is not decoration — imported rows never contribute to the engine accuracy panels, because we did not run the check that produced them.

Bulk actions, once rows are selected

Export selected · Add person to a list · Ask to resubmit · Delete session data. Anything that touches a person is an audited action with a required reason. Deleting is two-step: request, then execute by an admin, and it leaves a tombstone row so the counts on Home stay true.

How a session gets here

The engine puts it here. There is no manual “send to review” from the capture flow, because the customer should never be told to wait by a screen that has not run the checks yet.

Priority is set by channel, not by score: 1 POS check-in, 2 a live web session, 3 anything whose customer has already gone. Reviewing a priority-1 session out of order is fine and is not counted against anyone.

Liveness method
PASSIVE
Two seconds of frames, no instructions. Fastest, least annoying, weakest against a good replay attack. This is what runs today.
ACTIVE_3D
Head-turn prompts with a server nonce. Around 9 seconds. Much harder to replay; noticeably more drop-off at a busy counter.
FLASHING
A colour sequence flashed on the screen and matched in the reflection. Strong against printed and screen replays; unusable in bright daylight and a real problem for anyone photosensitive.
What is actually running now

PASSIVE for the score, plus a short challenge clip recorded with a server nonce, which is why the session page shows both a passive number and a challenge line. The challenge is checked for nonce match and injection signals even when the method is PASSIVE — that part is not optional.

Age rulethe one that matters here
REC_21 — recreational, 21 and over
Decided from the document date of birth. Under 21 is an automatic decline with UNDER_AGE. No estimator input.
MED_18_CARD — medical, 18 and over with a card
18 to 20 passes only when something says the customer is a medical patient. The engine can verify an age; it cannot verify a medical card.
Open question for you

MED_18_CARD needs a source of truth. Either the register passes a member flag with the session, or the customer photographs the card and an analyst looks at it. Those are different builds, and it is your call which one is real. Until you pick, this rule stays unselectable in production.

Customer-facing · not a console screen

The capture flow is the only part of Verify a customer ever sees. It is a phone, it is under a minute, and it never leaves someone staring at a screen that will not tell them what happened.

Two channels, one flow: a hosted link (verify.hyperwolf.com/v/<token>) that the site or an SMS opens, and the same steps mounted inside the register's check-in modal at the counter. The customer sees the same guides, the same copy and the same result either way; the difference is who is standing next to them.

Rules the flow obeys: the server decides, never the browser · nothing passes silently and nothing is dropped · every failure says what happens next · a hard signal declines without explaining the detection, because explaining it is a tutorial for the next attempt.

Why the retry copy blames the room

“Hold still, you moved” is a sentence a person reads as an accusation, and the second failure after an accusation is where they close the tab. Naming the light, the glare or the angle is both truer — those are what the scores actually reflect — and easier to act on.

What the customer is never told

Which model scored them, what the threshold was, or which signal fired on a hard decline. All of that is on the session page for your analysts. On the phone it would only teach the next attempt what to avoid.

When the engine is down at the counter

The photo becomes a template and the image is discarded. A match on a future session declines it with FACE_BLOCKLIST_HIT and names this list. Adding a person here is your call, never the engine's — nothing is auto-added to a blocklist.
Suspect devices — a custom list, not a blocklist

Devices that trigger INJECTION_DETECTED land on a custom list for review rather than being blocked outright. A shared tablet at a store is the most likely thing on it, and blocking a register would be a bad afternoon.

Hashing

Document numbers, emails and phone numbers are stored as a hash with a masked display value, so a stolen copy of the lists is not a list of your customers. IP entries hold CIDR text and countries hold codes, because both need to be matched by range.
