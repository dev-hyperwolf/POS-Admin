Design system review · 12 apps · light + dark

The system is sound.
The vocabulary is too large.

Nothing here is broken-looking — the editorial-operator direction reads well, dark mode is fully specified, and every screen already draws from one theme file. The problem is surface area: 30 colour slots, 30 button permutations, 19 type sizes, 8 radii and three parallel card/table/pill kits. That much choice guarantees drift, and drift is exactly what you're seeing when Engage feels like a different product. This audit trims the vocabulary and names the rules, without redrawing anything.

Reviewed POS · Orders · Members · Engage · Promotions · Pipeline · Delivery · Logistics · Driver · Terminals · @ Home · CRM Findings 21 Breaking changes 0

Keep — this is the good part

  1. One token file, honoured everywhere. Every app reads pos/tokens.jsx. That is rarer than it sounds and it's why this audit is cheap.
  2. Mono for all numerics. Money, counts, IDs and timestamps in JetBrains Mono is the single strongest thing about the UI — it makes an ops tool read like an instrument.
  3. Status is never colour-only. Pills carry a dot or an icon, so the colour-blind case already works.
  4. Warm paper + near-black rail. A distinctive, un-generic base that no competitor in this category has.

Fix — in this order

  1. Yellow is doing four jobs. Brand, primary action, selected state and "warm highlight". It should do one.
  2. No focus states anywhere. Zero :focus-visible in the React apps. Keyboard operators are currently flying blind.
  3. Touch targets below 44px. xs/sm buttons land at 23–29px on a counter touchscreen.
  4. Three of everything. Two pills, two stat tiles, two tables, two toasts — POS kit vs Engage's HD kit. That IS the cohesion gap.

The one-line diagnosis: the design system defines values (a palette, a radius list, a size list) but not roles (what yellow means, when a card is raised, which size is body text). Adding role tokens on top of the values you already have fixes most of this list without touching a single screen's layout.

A · Colour

30 named slots per mode, plus three parallel decorative sets. The values are fine; the assignments overlap.

1
Hyperwolf yellow carries four different meanings
Brand identity, the primary CTA, the selected/active state, and a generic "notice" wash. When one hue means four things, none of them read as urgent.
High
TENDER button → accent  ·  selected category chip → accent  ·  party total strip → accentSoft  ·  "New check-in" dashed tile → accentSoft  ·  VIP/tier pill → accentSoft  ·  AOV goal meter → accent  ·  rewards coins → accent

Fix. Reserve solid accent for the single most important action on screen — one per view, no exceptions. Selected states move to ink-filled (the primary treatment already in the kit). Informational washes move to a new neutral highlightSoft. Loyalty keeps yellow, because loyalty is the brand.

B · Buttons & controls

6 variants × 5 sizes = 30 permutations, no minimum height, no focus state, no loading state.

6
Sizes are padding-based, so real buttons land at 23px
On a counter touchscreen the accepted floor is 44px. xs and sm are used for destructive and confirming actions.
High
xs → 5px 8px @ 11.5px = ~23px tall  ·  sm → 7px 11px @ 12.5px = ~29px  ·  md → 9px 15px = ~33px  ·  lg → ~40px  ·  xl → ~48px ✓

Fix. Switch to min-height and cut to three sizes: 32 (dense/inline, mouse-only surfaces), 40 (default), 48 (touch primary). Keep the small end for admin consoles; require 40+ for anything a hand touches at the counter or in the van.

◢ Proposed — three, height-driven

Fewer, more distinct, and every one of them is hittable.
7
Six variants, and two of them mean "primary"
primary (ink) and accent (yellow) are both used as the confirming action, sometimes in the same dialog.
Medium
primary · accent · secondary · soft · ghost · danger  →  soft and secondary are visually 4% apart; danger is a secondary button with red text

Fix. Four variants: primary (accent — the one action), secondary (outline), ghost (text), danger (solid red — destructive should look destructive, not like a link). Retire soft; it's secondary with a fill. Ink-filled stays available as the selected treatment for chips and segments, not as a button variant.

8
No focus ring, no loading state, opacity-only disabled
A POS is operated by keyboard and barcode scanner as much as by touch. There is currently no way to see where focus is.
High

Fix. One focusRing token (2px surface gap + 2px info-blue) applied on :focus-visible in every interactive atom; a busy prop that swaps the label for a spinner and blocks double-submit (tender, refund and send-SMS all need it); and explicit disabled tokens instead of opacity:.45.

C · Cards, elevation & radius

Eight radii, three card implementations, and border-plus-shadow double delineation.

10
Cards carry a border AND a shadow
Two delineation systems doing one job makes dense screens look noisy and slightly muddy.
Medium

Fix. Pick one per elevation level: flat = border only (in-page cards, rows, panels), raised = shadow only, no border (popovers, sheets, dragged items), sunken = canvas2 fill, no border (wells, empty states). A card never has both.

12
Padding is an argument, so density is per-author
Card padding={18} is the default, but 0, 9, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16, 20 and 22 all appear in call sites.
Low

Fix. Replace the numeric prop with density="compact|default|roomy" → 12 / 16 / 20, and a 4px spacing scale for everything else (4 8 12 16 20 24 32). Authors stop guessing, and Engage's 20–24px rhythm and POS's 12–16px rhythm stop diverging by accident.

It isn't the colours. Engage draws from the same theme file. It's that Engage has its own component kit.

Engage runs a different page rhythm
24px page padding, 28–30px h1, 20px card padding vs POS's 22px / 21px / 12–16px. Same tokens, different music.
Medium

Fix. One page shell: 24px gutters, 24px between sections, 16px within a group, h1 at 30/800. 18
Empty, loading and error states are inconsistent or missing
The register has a nicely drawn empty cart; most tables just render nothing.
Medium

Fix. Three shared components — EmptyState (icon + one line + one action), Skeleton (the shimmer keyframe already exists in every app's CSS but is barely used), ErrorState (what failed, what to do, retry). Every list gets all three. This is the single most visible "unfinished" tell in a demo.

F · Accessibility & hardware reality

This UI runs on counter touchscreens, a phone in a van, and a manager's laptop. Only the laptop case is currently well served.

19
Interactive divs without roles or keyboard access
Order cards, table rows and product tiles are clickable <div>s — no tab stop, no Enter, no announced role.
High

Fix. Anything with onClick becomes a <button> (or gets role + tabIndex + key handler). The POS is a keyboard-and-scanner instrument; a cashier who can't tab to the tender button has to take a hand off the scanner.

20
Icon-only buttons without labels
IconBtn takes a title but many call sites omit it, and title isn't an accessible name on touch.
Medium

Fix. Make label required on IconBtn and emit it as aria-label; keep title for the mouse tooltip. Also give it a real 40px hit box — it has one, which is good, but the visual glyph should stay 16–18px inside it.

21
No reduced-motion path, and modals don't trap focus
Every overlay animates fade .15s; scrims close on click but don't return focus or block tab.
Low

Fix. One @media (prefers-reduced-motion) block per app that zeroes durations, and a shared Sheet/Modal primitive that owns focus trap, Escape, scroll lock and return-focus. Right now each modal re-implements the scrim and skips the rest.

G · Base scheme — answering “I don’t love it”

Two questions came back on the surface roles: should page and card swap, and what else could the workspace canvas be? Here is the diagnosis and four directions.

22
The flat feeling isn’t the colours — it’s a 4% step
Page #F4F2EC against card #FFFFFF is a 4% lightness difference. That is not enough for cards to read as objects, so a busy screen resolves into one continuous beige field.
High
bg #F4F2EC (L 95.6) → surface #FFFFFF (L 100) = 4.4 points
a comfortable card-on-page step is 7–10 points · a dense grid wants the upper end

So flipping is a real option, but it is not the only one — and flipping alone doesn’t fix the step, it just moves the warmth. All four directions below hold the same ink, the same rail and the same yellow; only the base changes.

Medium

Recommended2 · Warm greige

Same greige family as the page, two steps deeper. One temperature end to end; depth comes from lightness rather than hue. Pairs with scheme C.
