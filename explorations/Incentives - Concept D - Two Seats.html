Concept D — the organizing idea

Two audiences, two shells, one design system — switched by role, previewable by a manager.

A budtender and a floor manager want opposite things from the same ledger, so Bounty gives them deliberately different shells rather than one screen with sections hidden. The budtender seat is a single column that fits a 420px panel, so it can dock beside the register and still be read at arm’s length: touch-sized controls at P.ctrlH.lg/xl, big mono numbers, my rank with only the two people either side of me, my bounties as progress rows, my balance split into projected and settled, and the Snaps player running full-bleed in that same column. The manager seat is a console: a left inbox of things waiting on a person — pending approvals, unresolved identities, source errors, bounties ending today, settlements due — and a right workspace where standings, the builder, data runs and goals open as tabs, with a ⌘K palette that turns a sentence into a draft bounty.

It serves best the store that actually runs bounties: the budtender never scrolls past a builder they cannot use, and the manager never loses an approval inside a leaderboard. What it trades away is real — two layouts to maintain, and every new capability has to be answered twice or explicitly declined in one seat (the Data screen is manager-only, on purpose, and the seat says so instead of showing an empty tab). It also means a manager cannot see what their staff sees by accident, which is why the seat switch is a first-class control with a preview mode rather than a debug flag.

The shapes are borrowed, not invented. The budtender column takes its touch sizing and its top-strip rhythm from pos/screen-register.jsx — the same 44px row, the same 96px-tile scale, the same mono-metadata line. The inbox is pos/screen-home.jsx’s “Needs attention” card promoted to a column: identical row shape (30px icon plate · 12.5px/600 label · right-aligned action in P.info), identical empty line. The workspace tab strip is the Tabs atom, the palette is engage/app.jsx’s CommandPalette at ⌘K, and the manager panels keep AovManagerCard’s elevation="sunken" so a different audience’s panel recedes before you read its title.

What this concept trades away
  • Two layouts to maintain. Every screen answers the question twice, or declines once in writing. There is no shared “responsive” fallback doing both jobs badly.
  • A manager working the floor pays a toll. They switch seats to see what their own numbers look like. Preview is read-only, so the toll is one click, not a mode they can get stranded in.
  • The budtender seat has no Data, Builder or Goals. That is the point, and it is stated in the seat rather than implied by absence.
Standings — the same ledger, two readings

The budtender is told where they stand relative to two people. The manager is shown the whole table, plus what the table cannot see.

A rank is only useful to the person holding it if they can act on it, so the seat shows the gap to the person above and the gap from the person below and nothing else — the full table is one tap away, never in the way. The console gets the real table with period and metric controls, a per-source freshness line, the unattributed row, competition ranking with a tie marker, and the not-yet list under the fold of the table rather than as rank-last at zero.

Bounties — the console owns the lifecycle, the seat owns the progress

Every state is a real row in the list. The one that needs a person is also in the inbox, and the approval happens there or in the detail — the same action, two doors.

A brand-funded bounty cannot go live without a manager at the store approving it, so pending_approval is the only status that also occupies an inbox slot. active and ended are read off the clock rather than stored, which is why an ended bounty still needs a settle action before anyone is paid anything on paper — and why “ended” and “settled” are two different pills, never one.

Bounty builder — a sentence, then the form that sentence filled in

⌘K takes a sentence and produces a draft with every field it could infer filled and every field it could not named out loud.

The palette is engage/app.jsx’s CommandPalette with one extra behaviour: an unmatched query is parsed as a bounty rather than discarded. It never creates anything — it opens the builder pre-filled, at draft, with an explicit list of what it guessed and what it refused to guess. The form beneath is the whole model from §3.4: kind, metric, filter, window and recurrence, participants, reward, funding, tie rule.

1 · The palette, mid-query

⌘K anywhere in the console. Typing something that isn’t a destination switches the result area from “go to” to “create”.

Earnings — two numbers that must never merge

Projected is what the rules say you are on track for. Settled is what a manager has closed out. The seat shows both, always, and never adds them together.

Nothing in Bounty moves money. The ledger records earning, adjustment and a bookkeeping “recorded paid” marker that says who wrote it down and when — a note in a book, not a transfer. The seat leads with the split; the console gets the store ledger, the settlement action, and the marker.

Learn — Snaps in the seat, authoring in the console

The player is the one place the seat goes full-bleed: the story fills the 420px column edge to edge, because a card with a margin around a story reads as an advert someone else placed there.

Cards advance on tap — left third back, right two-thirds forward — with progress dots across the top in the shape everyone already knows. A quiz card is a card like any other, and passing it writes a real ledger row, which is why the reward card afterwards says what was written and where to see it. Authoring lives in the console: the card list, media, the quiz, an optional link to the bounty the snap is teaching, and the schedule.
