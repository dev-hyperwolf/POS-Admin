> **DRAFTING NOTE — READ BEFORE ADOPTION.** The instructing memorandum assumed that Civil Code
> § 1798.90.1 (i) imposes a 24-hour (or 72-hour) retention window and (ii) permits retention beyond
> that window upon the individual's consent. **Neither assumption is correct.** The operative text,
> as amended by Stats. 2018, ch. 548, § 1, contains **no** period of hours or days anywhere in the
> section and **no** consent exception applicable to a "business" (the only consent mechanism, at
> subd. (a)(2)(B), is confined to organ procurement organizations). The section operates instead as
> an absolute **purpose limitation**: subd. (a)(3) provides that a business "shall not retain or use
> any of the information obtained by that electronic means for any purpose other than as provided
> herein," and subd. (c) makes a violation a **misdemeanor** punishable by up to one year in a
> county jail and/or a $10,000 fine. Customer consent therefore **cannot** lawfully expand the
> permitted uses of information obtained by scanning a DMV-issued licence or identification card.
> The instrument below is drafted so that (A) all scan-derived licence data is confined to the four
> enumerated purposes of § 1798.90.1(a)(1), and (B) the "faster return visit" convenience purpose is
> carried **exclusively** by the selfie-derived Face Template and the account record, which are not
> "obtained by that electronic means" from the licence. See Part IV, Open Questions 1 and 2.

## IDENTITY VERIFICATION; RETENTION OF DRIVER'S LICENSE AND IDENTIFICATION CARD INFORMATION; BIOMETRIC INFORMATION

### 1. Definitions

1.1 **"Hyperwolf," "we," "us"** means the licensed commercial cannabis entity or entities operating under

Verification Session. **"Terms"** means the Hyperwolf Terms and Conditions, of which this insert forms a
part.

1.2 **"Identification Document"** means a valid form of identification of a class enumerated at title 4,
California Code of Regulations, section 15404, subdivision (b), namely: (a) a document issued by a federal,
state, county or municipal government (including a motor vehicle operator's licence) bearing the name, date
of birth, height, gender and photograph of the bearer; (b) a valid identification card issued to a member of
the Armed Forces bearing name, date of birth and photograph; or (c) a valid passport issued by the United
States or by a foreign government.

1.3 **"DMV Document"** means an Identification Document that is a driver's licence or identification card
issued by the California Department of Motor Vehicles, and to which Civil Code section 1798.90.1 applies.

1.4 **"Verification Session"** means a single instance of the Hyperwolf Verify process, conducted at account
sign-up, at in-store check-in, at the point of delivery, or upon re-verification.

1.5 **"DL Scan Data"** means all information obtained by electronic means from an Identification Document
during a Verification Session, comprising: (a) the digital photographic image of the front face of the
document; (b) the digital photographic image of the reverse face of the document; (c) the data string
decoded from the PDF417 two-dimensional barcode or equivalent machine-readable zone; and (d) the discrete
data elements extracted from (a) through (c), being full name, residence address, date of birth, document
number, issuing jurisdiction, date of issue, date of expiry, sex, height, eye colour, hair colour, document
discriminator, and the portrait image printed on the document.

1.6 **"Liveness Media"** means the selfie photograph and the short video sequence captured from you during a
Verification Session for the purpose of establishing that a live human being, and not a photograph, mask,
screen replay or synthetic likeness, is present at the point of capture.

1.7 **"Face Template"** means a mathematical representation, vector or template computed from the Liveness
Media that is capable of being used, singly or in combination with other identifying data, to establish or
confirm your individual identity. A Face Template constitutes "biometric information" within the meaning of
Civil Code section 1798.140, subdivision (c), and, when processed for the purpose of uniquely identifying
you, constitutes "sensitive personal information" within the meaning of Civil Code section 1798.140,
subdivision (ae)(2)(A). A Face Template also constitutes "unique biometric data" within the meaning of Civil
Code section 1798.81.5, subdivision (d)(1).

1.8 **"Face Match Score"** means the numeric similarity value computed between the portrait extracted from
the Identification Document and the Liveness Media.

1.9 **"Device Signals"** means the internet protocol address, device and browser identifiers, operating
system and application version, capture timestamp, coarse geolocation derived from internet protocol
address, and integrity and anti-tamper signals collected during a Verification Session.

1.10 **"Verification Decision Record"** means the durable record of the outcome of a Verification Session,
comprising: the outcome (approved, declined, or referred for human review); the date and time thereof; the
Face Match Score; the reason codes; the age-eligibility determination; the last four characters of the
document number; the issuing jurisdiction; the document expiry date; the version identifier of these Terms
and of the Retention Consent then in force; and the identity of any Hyperwolf personnel who conducted human
review. A Verification Decision Record does **not** include the images comprised in DL Scan Data.

1.11 **"Retention Consent"** means the separate, specific, unbundled affirmative authorisation described at
Section 5.3 and reproduced at Part II, Section B.

### 2. Purpose Limitation; Statutory Basis for Scanning

2.1 Hyperwolf scans, photographs and electronically reads Identification Documents solely for the purposes
enumerated at Civil Code section 1798.90.1, subdivision (a)(1), namely: (a) to verify your age or the

legal requirement to record, retain or transmit that information (§ 1798.90.1(a)(1)(B)); and (c) to collect
or disclose personal information required for reporting, investigating or preventing fraud, abuse or
material misrepresentation (§ 1798.90.1(a)(1)(D)).

2.2 Hyperwolf does not transmit information from an Identification Document to a check service company, and
accordingly does not rely on Civil Code section 1798.90.1, subdivision (a)(1)(C).

2.3 **HYPERWOLF SHALL NOT RETAIN OR USE ANY DL SCAN DATA FOR ANY PURPOSE OTHER THAN THOSE STATED AT SECTION
2.1.** This restriction is imposed by Civil Code section 1798.90.1, subdivision (a)(3), is not waivable by
you, and is not enlarged by any consent you may give. A violation constitutes a misdemeanour under Civil
Code section 1798.90.1, subdivision (c).

2.4 The underlying regulatory obligation that the verification satisfies is title 4, California Code of
Regulations, section 15404, subdivision (a) (retailer shall confirm the customer's age and identity by
inspecting a valid form of identification), applied to deliveries by title 4, California Code of
Regulations, section 15415, subdivision (d), and Business and Professions Code section 26140, subdivision
(a)(4) (no sale or transfer unless the purchaser first presents documentation reasonably appearing to be a
valid government-issued identification card).

2.5 You acknowledge that section 15404 requires **inspection** and does not itself require electronic
scanning, photographic capture, biometric comparison, or retention of any image. Hyperwolf's use of
Hyperwolf Verify is an additional fraud-prevention and record-integrity measure adopted at Hyperwolf's
election in reliance on Civil Code section 1798.90.1, subdivision (a)(1)(A) and (D).

# PART IV — MEMORANDUM TO CLIENT

**Privileged and confidential — attorney work product — DRAFT.** *(371 words)*

**What applies.** Five bodies of law. (1) **Civil Code § 1798.90.1** — the one that binds your
design. It lets you scan a DMV licence for four purposes only: age/authenticity, a legal record-keeping
requirement, a check-service transmission, and fraud prevention. Subdivision (a)(3) bars retention or use
for anything else. There is **no** 24-hour rule in the statute and **no** consent exception for businesses —
the 24-hour figure in the research brief is not in the text. Breach is a misdemeanour: up to a year in jail,
or $10,000 (subd. (c)). (2) **CCPA/CPRA** — your face template is biometric information (§ 1798.140(c)) and
sensitive personal information (§ 1798.140(ae)(2)(A)); you owe a notice at collection (§ 1798.100(a); 11 CCR
§ 7012(e)) and the rights at §§ 1798.105, .106, .110, .121. (3) **Civil Code § 1798.81.5(b)** — reasonable
security; biometric authentication data is expressly covered (subd. (d)(1)). (4) **B&P Code § 26140** and
**4 CCR § 15404** — the age check itself; note § 15404 requires *inspection*, not scanning.
(5) **B&P Code § 26160(b)** and **4 CCR § 15037(a)** — seven-year record retention. Illinois BIPA, Texas
CUBI and Washington's My Health My Data do not reach California-only operations, but would apply the moment
you verify a resident of those states — including online.

**What you must do.** Link these terms on the capture screen itself. Log every consent with
timestamp and version. Enforce the 24-hour purge automatically, not by policy. Honour deletion and limit
requests in 45 days. Complete a CCPA risk assessment before go-live — processing sensitive personal
information triggers it under 11 CCR § 7150(b)(2). Keep the human-review path staffed.

**Open questions I need to resolve.**

1. **The convenience purpose is the exposure.** "Faster return visits" is not one of § 1798.90.1's four
   purposes. The draft solves this by building the returning-customer path on the *selfie* template, never
   on licence-derived data. Confirm the engineering matches before launch.
2. Whether § 1798.90.1 (written for stripe/barcode swipes in 2003) reaches camera capture of a licence.
   Assume yes; no case says otherwise.
3. Whether cannabis retail is a "significant decision" under the ADMT rules (11 CCR §§ 7200-7222,
   compliance 1 April 2027). The final list is finance, housing, education, employment, healthcare — so
   probably not. Section 12 gives the disclosure and appeal anyway.
4. Confirm the 36-month template cap and the 90-day investigation tail against your actual fraud caseload.
